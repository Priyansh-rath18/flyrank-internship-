FlyRank ML Internship — Capstone Field Report

Content Archetypes for Content Review Prioritization

A descriptive, reproducible survey of published content — classified by search visibility, engagement, authority, and content-level signals.

Collector: Priyansh Srivastava
Lane: Data Science / ML
Method: K-Means, standardized
Status: Decision-support
Plate I

Abstract

This study asks whether published content can be grouped into useful performance archetypes to support content-review prioritization. The analysis uses the public FlyRank ML Internship dataset, combining content attributes with search and website-performance aggregates. DuckDB and Pandas were used for data preparation and feature engineering, followed by standardized K-Means clustering. The six-cluster analysis produced descriptive groups named Star Performer, Authority Pillar, Visible Underconverter, Buried Thin Page, Hidden Engagement Gem, and Dormant, with proposed actions such as Protect, Improve, Rewrite, Merge, Monitor, and Prune. The output is a directional decision-support framework for helping editors prioritize content review, not a proven prediction of future rankings or traffic.

Plate II

1. Problem framing

Content teams may have many published pages but limited time to decide which pages deserve review first. A single metric, such as impressions, can miss other useful signals such as engagement, ranking position, backlinks, and content depth.

Research question

Can published content be grouped into meaningful performance archetypes using observed search, engagement, authority, and content-level signals?

Unit of analysis

A published content item identified by the pseudonymous content_hash_id.

Output

A cluster assignment, descriptive archetype, proposed action, and cluster-level performance profile.

Human decision

An editor uses the output to prioritize manual checks, not to make automatic content changes.

An incorrect recommendation could cause an unnecessary rewrite, an unsafe consolidation, the removal of useful content, or a missed improvement opportunity.

3. Baseline

The proposed baseline ranks eligible content by 90-day impressions, from highest to lowest. A fixed top fraction or fixed number of pages is then selected for review.

This is a transparent comparison because impressions are easy to understand and reproduce. The baseline and clustering-based method must use the same content population, selection budget, evaluation window, and outcome metric.

Plate V

4. Model / analysis

The analysis uses standardized K-Means clustering to group content according to observed search, engagement, authority, and content-related features.

Workflow

  1. Load the three Parquet tables with DuckDB.
  2. Aggregate query-level and daily performance by content_hash_id.
  3. Join performance aggregates with content attributes.
  4. Filter to published, non-deleted content.
  5. Engineer ratios, momentum, and log-transformed features.
  6. Standardize the feature matrix using StandardScaler.
  7. Evaluate candidate cluster counts from 4 through 7 using silhouette score.
  8. Fit a six-cluster K-Means model.
  9. Inspect cluster profiles and assign descriptive archetype names.

Feature list

log_impressions · log_clicks · ctr_90d · avg_position_90d_w · momentum · query_count · rare_impressions_share_avg · engagement_rate · avg_engagement_sec · log_scroll_events · log_backlinks · log_search_volume · word_count

5. Evaluation

The current notebook evaluates internal cluster structure using silhouette scores and examines cluster profiles. It does not yet establish future predictive performance.

Recommended time-aware design

  1. Build features from an earlier performance window.
  2. Fit the scaler and clustering model using that earlier window.
  3. Assign content to archetypes.
  4. Measure clicks, impressions, or engagement in a separate future window.
  5. Compare the clustering-based priority rule with the impressions baseline.
  6. Use the same selection budget and future outcome metric for both methods.

Plate VII

6. Interpretation

The six-cluster solution separates content into groups with different observed combinations of visibility, ranking, engagement, authority, and content-related signals.

Star Performer

Interpreted as strong observed performance. Proposed action: Protect.

Authority Pillar

Interpreted as strong authority or visibility with possible improvement opportunities. Proposed action: Improve.

Visible Underconverter

Interpreted as visible content with weaker click performance relative to exposure. Proposed action: Rewrite.

Buried Thin Page

Interpreted as limited visibility with weaker content-depth signals. Proposed action: Review for Merge.

Hidden Engagement Gem

Interpreted as useful engagement despite limited search visibility. Proposed action: Monitor.

Dormant

Interpreted as limited observed activity. Proposed action: Review for Prune.

Visual evidence

These figures show observed distributions and relationships. They do not establish causation, prove future performance, or predict Google's ranking algorithm.

Plate VIII

7. Recommendation

The following playbook is a proposed operational review order. It is not a measured ranking of business impact.

OrderArchetype — actionWhat to check
1Visible Underconverters — RewriteSearch intent, titles, descriptions, page openings, and whether the content matches the likely user need.
2Star Performers — ProtectMonitor clicks, impressions, ranking, and engagement. Avoid unnecessary changes.
3Authority Pillars — ImproveCompleteness, internal linking, related-topic coverage, and intent alignment.
4Buried Thin Pages — Merge candidatesUnique purpose, valuable links, duplication, and whether consolidation is appropriate.
5Hidden Engagement Gems — MonitorOther traffic sources, internal navigation, and whether the page serves a narrow but valuable audience.
6Dormant Pages — Prune candidatesBusiness importance, backlinks, historical value, and whether refreshing or redirecting is better than removal.

Filter the action playbook, inspect a small sample from each archetype, check business and technical context, record the human decision, and measure the result in a future period.
